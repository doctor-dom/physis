Here is a detailed guide on **adjusting GIR calculations when combining IV dextrose with enteral feeds**, as well as when using concentrated formulas.

**Combined IV + Enteral GIR Calculation**

When a neonate is receiving both IV dextrose and enteral feeds, the **total GIR is the sum of the IV GIR and the enteral GIR**:

$$\text{Total GIR} = \text{IV GIR} + \text{Enteral GIR}$$

Each component is calculated separately:

**IV GIR:**

$$\text{IV GIR (mg/kg/min)} = \frac{\text{Dextrose concentration (\%)} \times \text{IV rate (mL/hr)} \times 10}{60 \times \text{weight (kg)}}$$

For example, D10W at 6 mL/hr in a 3 kg infant:

$$\frac{10 \times 6 \times 10}{60 \times 3} = \frac{600}{180} = 3.3 \text{ mg/kg/min}$$

**Enteral GIR:**

$$\text{Enteral GIR (mg/kg/min)} = \frac{\text{CHO (g/100 mL)} \times \text{feed rate (mL/hr)} \times 10}{60 \times \text{weight (kg)}}$$

For example, Similac Advance 20 kcal/oz (7.2 g CHO/100 mL) at 15 mL/hr in a 3 kg infant:

$$\frac{7.2 \times 15 \times 10}{60 \times 3} = \frac{1080}{180} = 6.0 \text{ mg/kg/min}$$

**Total GIR** = 3.3 + 6.0 = **9.3 mg/kg/min**

This combined approach is critical during the transition from IV to enteral nutrition, as the enteral GIR must be accounted for when weaning IV dextrose to avoid both hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia. The AAP, ABM, SNG, and other guidelines recommend maintaining a total GIR of **5–8 mg/kg/min** for neonatal hypoglycemia management, with stepwise increases up to 10–13.3 mg/kg/min if needed for refractory cases.[1][2]

**Adjusting for Concentrated Formulas**

When formulas are concentrated above the standard 20 kcal/oz (0.67 kcal/mL), the CHO content per mL increases proportionally. The scaling factor is straightforward:

$$\text{CHO at target cal density} = \text{CHO at 20 kcal/oz} \times \frac{\text{target kcal/oz}}{20}$$

**Important practical notes:**

- The proportional scaling above assumes concentration is achieved by adding less water to powder. When using **liquid concentrate or modular additives** (e.g., Polycose, MCT oil), the CHO content may differ from simple proportional scaling — Polycose adds carbohydrate without proportionally increasing other macronutrients, while MCT oil adds calories without carbohydrate, effectively lowering the CHO-to-calorie ratio.
- When **weaning IV dextrose**, the enteral GIR should be recalculated at each feed advancement. A common approach is to decrease the IV dextrose rate by the equivalent GIR being gained from enteral feeds, maintaining a stable total GIR. For example, if advancing feeds from 5 to 10 mL/hr with a formula providing 72 mg/mL CHO in a 3 kg infant, the enteral GIR increases by approximately 2 mg/kg/min, and the IV rate can be reduced accordingly.[4][5]
- For **bolus feeds** rather than continuous feeds, the GIR calculation uses the average hourly rate over the feeding interval (e.g., 30 mL q3h = 10 mL/hr average). However, the actual glucose delivery is pulsatile, which may result in glycemic variability compared to continuous feeds. Some NICU protocols use continuous drip feeds specifically for infants with refractory hypoglycemia to provide a more stable GIR.[6]
- The neonatal glucose utilization rate is approximately **5–8 mg/kg/min**, which forms the physiologic basis for GIR targets across multiple international guidelines. For infants with congenital hyperinsulinism or glycogen storage disease, higher GIR targets (up to 10–15+ mg/kg/min) may be necessary.[1][6]
